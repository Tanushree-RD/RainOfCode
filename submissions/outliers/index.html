Train the classifier

Two classifiers run at detection time: a 1D convolutional neural network trained right here in your browser on synthetic transit/non-transit light curves, and a large language model (Llama, via Groq) that reasons over the measured features in text. Their probabilities are averaged.

How this works

The CNN reads the raw, normalized light curve directly (real convolutional filters, real backpropagation, real train/validation split — sized to however many points are currently loaded) and learns to recognize the transit shape on its own. The LLM instead reads ~20–32 hand-measured numbers (depth, symmetry, gas amplitudes, etc.) as text and reasons about them. At detection time, if both are ready their probabilities are averaged; if only one is ready, that one is used alone.
